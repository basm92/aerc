6  The Effect of Historical Treatment on Population Density: France-Germany Border Region

6.1 Introduction

This notebook analyses the relationship between a historical treatment variable and present-day population density in the France-Germany border region. The dataset contains LAU-level regions straddling the French-German border, with a binary indicator (treatment) marking which regions received the historical treatment of interest.

We will:

  1. Load and map the spatial data.
  2. Filter to regions with a valid treatment assignment.
  3. Estimate a bivariate OLS regression of population density on the treatment indicator.
  4. Display the results in a publication-style table.

6.3 Step 2: Filter to regions with a treatment assignment

Some regions have NA for treatment — these are regions where treatment status is unknown or not applicable (e.g. regions far from the border). We drop them so that all three models are estimated on the same sample.

data <- fr_gr |>
  filter(!is.na(treatment))

6.4 Step 3: Estimate the regression model

We use fixest::feols() to estimate a bivariate OLS regression of population density on the treatment indicator.

library(fixest)

m1 <- fixest::feols(POP_DENS_2016 ~ treatment, data = data)
NOTE: 511 observations removed because of NA values (LHS: 511).
